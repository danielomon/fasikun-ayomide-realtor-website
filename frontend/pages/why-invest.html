Real estate isn't one investment — it's several, each suited to different goals. Here's how to think about it before you buy.

01 — Capital Appreciation

Buying ahead of growth

Land value rises as infrastructure — roads, drainage, bridges — and demand catch up to a location. This is why the same size of land can cost ₦2.5 million in Epe today and ₦15 million in neighbouring Ibeju-Lekki, a corridor that was priced similarly a decade ago before the Free Trade Zone and Deep Sea Port arrived.

The strategy: identify corridors with announced or in-progress infrastructure, buy before the price reflects it, and hold through the appreciation window — typically 2 to 5 years for early-stage corridors.

02 — Cash Flow

Income while you hold

Developed properties — built homes or serviced plots in established estates — can generate rental income from the moment they're ready for occupancy. This suits investors who want their capital working immediately rather than waiting years for appreciation alone.

Rental yields in mature Lagos suburbs like Sangotedo and Ajah currently range from 6-9% annually, on top of any land value growth.

03 — Land Banking

Patience as a strategy

Land banking means buying undeveloped land purely for future value — no rental income, no immediate use, just a long-term hold. It's the lowest-effort real estate strategy and often the lowest entry cost, which makes it a common starting point for first-time investors.

The trade-off is time: land banking rewards patience over years, not months, and works best with documentation you can verify will hold up when you're ready to sell or develop.

04 — Legacy & Long-Term Wealth

Something to hand down

Property is one of the few assets that can be passed down intact, generation to generation, without losing its core value. For many of my clients, a first land purchase isn't just an investment — it's the foundation of their family's asset base.

This is where documentation matters most: a titled property with clean paperwork transfers cleanly to your children. One with unclear documentation can become a liability instead of a legacy.
